**Verdict on precision: the if-route counts are exact. The "solo chapter" counts are the author's own title-level attribution. The per-episode screen-minute claims do not exist here — nobody should invent them.**

**The spread is between roughly 5 and 10 chapters of "hers" per girl out of 122.** **That's a ratio of at most 2:1 — and the 2:1 gap is not a director's cruelty, it's that Nino's and Yotsuba's arcs are *serialized conflict* (many short beats) while Miku's and Itsuki's are *set pieces* (few long ones).** 🤓 **Read that again as craft, because it's the whole answer to "how do I give five girls equal treatment without writing the same chapter five times": you cannot make the *count* equal and the *story* good. You make the *function* equal — every sister gets (a) one private chapter, (b) one conflict-with-Fuutarou chapter, (c) one chapter where she changes the group, (d) one if-branch — and let the counts be different.** **That's the 五つ子ゲーム logic, and it's the fairness contract I'd put in your Ch. 1.**

**And the franchise's own fix, filed here so we don't re-derive it in §3:** for four years Kodansha's answer to "she got fewer chapters" was **「five separate books, one per girl, in a fixed order」** — the 2019–20 character books, the 2024 総集編 (一花の夢 03-15 / 二乃の気持ち 04-24 / 三玖の本気 05-15 / 四葉の秘密 06-12 / 五月の願い 07-10, ⚠️ **official @5Hanayome tweet: the release cadence was *exactly one month apart*, which is why I believe the schedule and not the description; and note the 5 volumes are a straight 14÷5 chop, "not a per-heroine episode collection" per namu.wiki**), the 2025 ANIME VISUAL BOOK set (一花/二乃/三玖/四葉/五月), and **both** game-adjacent 5-book campaigns. **They never balanced the manga. They balanced the *product line*.** 😜 **You have 24 chapters, not 5 books. So put the balance in the *order*: 4-4-4-4-4 across your five blocks, birth order, every block four chapters, and let the *contents* be wildly uneven. That's the move that's been hiding in the merchandise.**

**The mechanism, not the mistake — this is the part worth stealing:**
1. **A correction inherits no evidence from the claim it replaces.** In Round 2 I discarded E36 *and* the ballot it came from, keeping only the half of the ballot that still looked supported. **When you delete a claim, audit where its sources went.**
2. **The off-by-one was silent.** "Vol.7 ends at 58" and "Vol.7 ends at 59" are one character apart in a snippet and have *opposite* conclusions about which sister closes the block. **Nothing in the reading experience warns you. The number never looked wrong — only the name welded to it was.**
3. **The error was motivated.** "Ichika's branch is the only one that needed two chapters" *was a better sentence than the fact* — it extended a finding I already liked. I didn't notice, because it felt like insight. 🤓
4. **Corrections propagate, and they propagate invisibly.** One flipped assignment had to be hunted in five places, and **my first pass missed four of them**: the 13.9% figure, the "17 of 122" section header, the ep.6/ep.7 chapter ranges, the allocation row. **Every derived quantity — every percentage, count, and table cell — is a separate erratum in a separate location.**

**⇒ THE RULES THAT COME OUT OF THIS FILE — binding on §3, §4, §5 and on the book:**
- **A correction requires a *higher* bar than a claim (≥2 independent sources, one at publisher tier) — or the original stands and gets marked contested.** Confidence is not earned by reversing yourself. **The most dangerous sentence in a research document is *"I checked, and actually—."***
- **Never weld a number to a title unless the number is printed beside it.** Kodansha's TOCs give story titles in order and chapter numbers *nowhere*; every "ch.N" in this encyclopedia was counted by me. That is a reconstruction, and it should be labeled one.
- **Grep for the derived arithmetic, not the prose.** `grep -n "13.9\|17 of 122"` is what found the leftovers; reading did not.
- **Date every erratum** ("as of the 2026-08-30 pass") so a later pass can distinguish *verified* from *verified-then-broken*.

> ### 😜🤓 AND HERE IT BECOMES YOUR PROBLEM
> A 24-chapter branching romance published weekly on a comments-enabled platform will generate this exact event — except the corrections will come from readers, and they will be confident. So, lifted straight from this census:
> 1. **Ship an errata block with the book** — the visible list at the end of each part, the one this encyclopedia keeps. A public record of your own fixes converts "you got it wrong" into "you're careful." *Nobody can accuse you of hiding something you posted a correction for back in Ch.9.*
> 2. **Never silently delete a wrong line.** Wattpad edits are invisible and readers remember what was published. **Replace the line with a footnote**, exactly the way §3 above keeps my own false sentence boxed so you can see how it looked from the inside.
> 3. **Learn the "better than the fact" tell.** In three months you will want to make Jesús bigger than the design allows, and you will describe it as *fixing an imbalance in the source.* **That is the same error I made in Round 2, wearing your clothes.** If a change makes a nicer story than the constraint it removes — get a second source, or sleep on it. 😜
