## 1. 🔒 The Battle of Puebla (5 May 1862) — and the fact nobody tells you
**~2,000–4,000 Mexican troops defeat a French force roughly double/triple their size, Napoleon III's invasion, General Ignacio Zaragoza.** 🔒 [history.com](https://www.history.com/articles/cinco-de-mayo-history) / [rd.com expert Q&A](https://www.rd.com/article/cinco-de-mayo-history/).
**⚔️ THEN: France occupied Mexico City from 1863 to 1867 and installed a puppet empire. The battle was won. The war was lost. Maximilian was executed in 1867.**
🤓 **So his birthday is the anniversary of a tactical victory followed by five years of occupation.** 🔥 **And in this property, five years is the exact measure of the hole in every household: 「本編開始時(2017年9月)から5年前に亡くなっており」 — Rena died five years before page one (§3 / [pixiv 中野零奈](https://dic.pixiv.net/a/中野零奈)).** ⚔️ **Jesús walks into the Nakano house and wins a small, improbable round against 二乃 on day one — and is then living under an occupation for the next five years of the story. That's the shape of the whole book and it's already printed on his birth certificate.**

## 2. 🔥🔥 THE HOLIDAY WAS MADE ABROAD — this is the one to build on
🔒 **It is not a federal holiday in Mexico; for most Mexicans 「May 5 is a day like any other: offices, banks and stores remain open」; observance is centered in Puebla** [history.com](https://www.history.com/articles/is-cinco-de-mayo-celebrated-in-mexico). **And: 「the Cinco de Mayo holiday took shape in 1863 in the United States」 — Mexican residents of California began commemorating it the following year, raising money for Juárez's troops and recruiting men, explicitly tying it to the fight against the Confederacy during the U.S. Civil War** [history.com](https://www.history.com/articles/cinco-de-mayo-history) · [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/article/cinco-de-mayo-celebration.html). **It faded in Mexico after Díaz (who had fought at Puebla), was revived by the Chicano movement in the 1960s–70s, and got its modern commercial mass from FDR's Good Neighbor Policy and then beer marketing in the '70s–'80s.** 🔒 [rd.com](https://www.rd.com/article/cinco-de-mayo-history/) — ⚠️ *"it became a giant advertisement for beer" is a Reddit commenter's phrasing, not the historian's; the beer-marketing claim itself is sourced.*
> ⚔️ **THE SENTENCE: Cinco de Mayo is a Mexican holiday that is more celebrated by people who are not in Mexico. It exists more strongly in the country he is leaving than in the one he is arriving at — and Japan, being a third party, only knows it as a beer commercial and a Taco Bell menu.**
> 🔥 **That is 「ni de aquí, ni de allá」 — which Kevin already wrote as the theme of §13 — as a calendar fact rather than a feeling.** ⚠️ **The book does not need to say it. The book needs ONE scene where a classmate congratulates him on his national day and he says "it's not my national day," and then, alone, realizes that in the United States he's right, and in Japan he's the strange one, and in Mexico he has nothing to explain at all — because nobody there cares.**

## 4. 🔥🔥 子供の日 (May 5) — Japan's law about his birthday, in Japanese
🔒 **The statutory text of 国民の祝日 as 子供の日: 「こどもの人格を重んじ、こどもの幸福をはかるとともに、母に感謝する」** — *to respect children as individuals, to wish for their happiness, **and to be grateful to one's mother**.* [MAHOROBA](https://shop.mahoroba-inter.com/blogs/journal/gogatsu_02) · [東玉](https://www.tougyoku.com/gogatsu-ningyou/gogatsu-ningyou-column/kodomonohi/kodomonohi-matome/) (established 1948 / 昭和23年).
> ⚔️ **His mother died on his birthday.** 🔥 **The state mandated gratitude to the mother on the exact date the boy lost hers — and the day is also, in its older form, 端午の節句, a boy's rite: the helmet and armor 兜・鎧 set out to guard a son from illness and disaster.** 🔒 **The 兜's whole meaning is "something hard placed over a boy so that he is not killed."** ⚠️ *The property has already used this object once — the 兜 is the Nakano sisters' own* origin *motif territory via the mother, and I have not verified a helmet appears in QQ; if it does not, don't force it.*
> **Handle, and it's tiny: do not write a Children's Day chapter. Write a scene where he is handed a 柏餅 he does not understand and eats it in the wrapper, badly, on the worst day of the book.**

## 5. 🔥🔥 THE KOINOBORI ARE FIVE COLOURS AND THEY ARE AN OFFERING FOR A CORPSE
🔒 **The ちまき origin, verbatim: the minister 屈原 drowned himself on 5 May; people threw rice into the river so his body would not be eaten by the dragon-fish; the dragon disliked 茅の葉, so the rice was wrapped in leaves and tied with 五色の糸 — 「※ちなみに、このときの五色の糸が鯉のぼりの色となっているともいわれます」** [tamaplaza](https://tamaplaza-washoku-zuitei.com/blog/2020-04-09/端午の節句由来、歴史-子供の日-柏もち/) (hedged as 「ともいわれます」, ⚠️ *so: 🔸-tier folk etymology, exactly the tier the property's own legends live at*).
⚔️ **Five colours, tied around a leaf-wrapped food, to keep a dead body from being consumed by a dragon — hoisted above a boy so he will grow up strong.**
> 🔥🔥 **The Nakano quintuplets ARE the five carp.** ⚔️ *And Jesús arrives on May 5 as the sixth streamer hoisted over a house whose mother's body the family has been trying, for five years, not to let be eaten.* **That is not a decorative parallel. That is the entire mythology of his adoption day, already written by a culture he was born into but never lived in, and he can pass through all of it without understanding one thing — because a Japanese boy of 16 would not know the 屈原 story either.**
> **⚠️ Handling rule: ONE image. Never the explanation. If a character explains 五色の糸 on the page, the book becomes a footnote with dialogue.**

## 6. 🔥 THE OAK LEAF — 「柏の葉は新芽が出てから古い葉が落ちる」⇒「家系が絶えない」「子孫繁栄」
🔒 **The oak does not shed its old leaves until the new buds have come out — which is why 柏餅 wrapped in it is an omen of an unbroken line** [hibiyakadan](https://www.hibiyakadan.com/item/LIFESTYLE_Z_0037.html) · [hoshinonaruki](https://hoshinonaruke.jp/gosekku/906/) · [tamaplaza](https://tamaplaza-washoku-zuitei.com/blog/2020-04-09/...). **Also 🔒: it's mostly a Kantō custom (oak grows poorly in western Japan, so Kansai eats ちまき) — ⚔️ and Aichi is the seam between the two, which means Kevin's setting is precisely the region where the leaf and the rice-ball traditions meet.**
> ### ⚔️ THIS IS THE ANSWER TO 馬流.
> **The grandfather's whole canonical problem is that his son-in-law-less mother line had no heir he could hold: 「実父である中野無堂仁之介は、零奈が五つ子を妊娠した時点で消息を絶っています」 — no new bud, and he has been holding dead leaves on the tree ever since (§A: he became the tutor's financier and the father who 「could not tell his own granddaughters apart」).**
> 🔥 **In the oak's own logic: the old leaf is not *stubborn*, it is *waiting for the sprout.* And on 5 May a boy with 藤 in his name — 藤 = a vine that climbs what it grows on — walks in.** ⚠️ **Do not have anyone say this. Have 馬流 watch him eat a 柏餅 with the leaf still on it, and have 馬流 say nothing at all for one panel-equivalent, and then give him the second one.**
> ⚔️ **And the leaf-motif converges with Kevin's own text:** 四**葉** = four leaves · 「Mazapanes **de la Rosa**」 — 🔒 a real product, and 🔥 **the wrapper is part of the candy; "the class destroys them trying to unwrap it" is a true detail** · the sister's name is 五**月** = the fifth month = the day itself. ⚔️ **Three wrapped-in-a-leaf foods at one table on Children's Day: paper (memory), oak (lineage), bamboo grass (a corpse defended).** *That is the sort of page readers make 4,000-word posts about. It must be played completely straight.*

- ☠️ **「Cinco de Mayo = Mexican Independence Day」.** ✅ Kevin already knows — and ⚔️ **he wrote a football girl who doesn't, which is the correct use of the error: it is character, not author.**

If 一花 ever explains it, I will be disappointed in you.**
- ⚠️ **Over-stuffing risk (the real one).** **You now have: a lost war, a holiday invented abroad, a hero born on the wrong side of a border, a mother-gratitude law, five carp colors made from a funeral offering, and an oak leaf that waits for an heir.** 🤓 **That is six theses and a novel has room for two.** **My ranking: (1) holiday-invented-abroad, (2) the oak leaf / heir — because those two are about *him*. The koinobori is about the family, so it's scenery and scenery is allowed. The rest, cut.**
